METHOD & SOURCES

Same definition.
Same reference year.

The map and comparison statistics use GHSL–OECD estimated functional urban areas, GHS-FUA R2019A: urban centers and their modeled commuting zones in 2015. International search includes regions with at least 500,000 residents in that dataset.

Why the years differ: The U.S. selector uses the 30 largest metropolitan statistical areas from the July 2025 Census estimates. Each selection is matched to a 2015 global functional region. The current Census population is used for ranking only; every comparison card, population suggestion, and export uses the matched region’s 2015 population and area.

Boundaries matter. These are not official MSA boundaries or measurements of developed land. Some U.S. metros share a larger functional region: Los Angeles and Riverside use the same source region; San Francisco is represented by the source’s San Jose region. San Diego uses the U.S. portion of the source’s Tijuana region. Such mappings are flagged beside the selector and named in the comparison table and PNG.

*How scale is preserved: Source region centers are area-weighted centroids computed in the original equal-area Mollweide coordinate system. Foreign boundaries are moved by retaining each vertex’s spherical distance and bearing from its original center. This corrects latitude effects instead of copying Web Mercator pixels. North stays up; small shape and area differences from spherical transplantation are possible. Shapes are never resized to equalize population.

Imperial units are the default: miles, square miles, and square feet. Metric units remain available using the unit toggle. Area per resident is total regional land area divided by modeled population. It includes rural land. These maps can illustrate settlement patterns but do not establish transport emissions or environmental performance by themselves.

PNG export: Exports the current visible map at 2× resolution, with visible regions, legend, statistics, scale, and source notes. Street tiles require an internet connection. Missing tiles are identified in the exported image; region geometry remains available.
